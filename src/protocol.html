Botanical Protocol

A daily herbal drink within safety limits: ingredients studied for antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic benefits

An herbal routine: a warm spiced drink once a day, capsules after it, and charcoal at bedtime. Enter your weight below to get your amounts.

For education only. Not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before starting, especially if you take medicine or have a health condition. You use this information at your own risk.

Before you start

  • Short-term only:
    • Sage: 2 weeks at most. Source 4
    • Berberine: France's food safety agency assessed it for up to 14 days and says its long-term safety can't be guaranteed. Source 7
    • Activated charcoal: an emergency medicine that can also stop medicines from being absorbed. Sources 19, 20
  • Skip it if you take blood thinners or any medicine, unless a pharmacist or doctor says it's OK. Turmeric, ginger, and garlic each thin the blood a little, and the effects add up, so mention it before any surgery or dental work. Sources 7, 12, 13, 15
  • Not for pregnancy, breastfeeding, or children. Sources 7, 14

How to make it

Once a day. Take it with or without food.

Your serving · in the cup
  • 1 tspturmeric powder
  • ½ tspblack pepper
  • ⅛ tspcayenne pepper
  • ½ tspginger powder
  • ¼ tspclove powder
  • ⅛ tspsage powder Short-term
  • 1 tbspsunflower lecithin (granules or powder)
  • 1 tbspolive oil (or coconut oil)
  • 1 tspblack seed oil
  • 1 cupwarm water, about 100°F (38°C)
  • 1 clovefresh garlic
Right after the drink
  • 300 mgberberine Short-term
  • 2 softgelsoregano oil (or as the label says)
At bedtime
  • 2 capsulesactivated charcoal, with a full glass of water Short-term

Amounts match the weight entered above.

  1. Crush the garlic. Crush 1 clove and let it sit for 10 minutes. Researchers suggest this wait lets more of garlic's active compounds form. Source 18
  2. Combine the powders. Put the powders and sunflower lecithin from your serving list in a cup.
  3. Make the slurry. Add the olive (or coconut) oil and black seed oil with a splash of the warm water. Stir into a smooth paste.
  4. Add warm water. Pour in the rest of about 1 cup of warm water, ideally from a tea kettle, at about 100°F (38°C). It should feel warm, not hot, on your wrist. Stir well.
  5. Add the garlic. Chop the rested garlic and stir it into the warm drink.
  6. Drink right away. Stir as you drink, since the powders sink quickly.
  7. Take the capsules. After the slurry, swallow the berberine capsule and oregano oil softgels whole.
  8. At bedtime, take the charcoal. Swallow the activated charcoal capsules with a full glass of water. Keep them at least 2 hours away from the morning dose and any medicines, since charcoal can stop them from being absorbed. Sources 19, 20

Cautions

  • If it upsets your stomach, take it with food. France's food safety agency lists stomach upset as a berberine risk. Source 7
  • Charcoal turns stools black, which can hide black stools caused by stomach bleeding. Watch instead for dizziness, weakness, stomach pain, or vomit that looks like blood or coffee grounds. Sources 19, 21
  • Stop and get checked for yellow skin or eyes (a possible sign of liver trouble), or severe stomach pain or vomiting. Sources 12, 14, 30
What the research shows

Most of this research is lab or animal work. It hasn't shown that this protocol treats infections in people. Lab results often use far stronger amounts than reach the body after swallowing.

Human tested in people   Animal tested in mice or rats   Lab tested on germs or cells in a dish

Berberine

  • ParasitesHumanTwo early non-randomized studies (1972, 1975) reported activity against Giardia, a gut parasite. A later review rated the giardiasis research overall as very poor quality. Source 32
  • ParasitesLabDamaged Giardia, Entamoeba, and Trichomonas parasites in lab cultures. Source 33
  • VirusesAnimalReduced flu virus in infected mice and in cell cultures. Source 34
  • BacteriaHumanRandomized trial, 165 adults: a single 400 mg dose reduced stool volume in E. coli diarrhea. The effect on cholera was slight. Source 35
  • BacteriaHumanReview of 13 trials (2,048 people): adding berberine to standard antibiotics raised H. pylori cure rates. The reviewers rated the trials' quality as low. Source 36
  • FungiLabMade fluconazole (an antifungal drug) work again against resistant Candida yeast in lab tests. Source 37
  • BiofilmLabBlocked Staph aureus biofilm behavior in lab tests. Source 38

Other studied benefits

These are findings from studies of single ingredients, not of this protocol. Human trials mostly lasted 8 to 12 weeks or longer, often used concentrated extracts at higher doses, and studied people who already had the condition. Animal and lab results often use far stronger amounts than reach the body. Kitchen amounts taken for a short time aren't expected to produce the same results.

How the estimates work

Spices are measured by volume, but limits are set for one active compound by weight. So each spoonful is converted to grams, then to an estimated amount of that compound. Spice strength varies a lot between batches, so each estimate shows a low–high range, with a tick at the typical value.

Reading the bar: the dashed line is the limit for the weight entered. The shaded band is the likely range for the amount entered.
